My philosophy around items is that they need to be unique but similar in their power (at a baseline). The difference between the best iron axe and the worst iron axe is how quickly it breaks + how easily it can cut, but it can't suddenly penetrate steel because it's sharp.

Durability systems should be based around the lowest possible rolls. This way, damage to a weapon can happen even if you do something skillfully. in that same vein, on a crit no damage would be taken by a weapon (at the GM's discretion). 

Damage should be based on the class of an item + additional modifiers such as sharpness or deftness. Anything else should be an ability (Iron Axe - Cleave vs. Mithril Axe - *Wondrous* Cleave)

What I like about items from video games:
- Enhancing what items ***do*** past the regular bounds
- Item differences that are unique and measured (see cloak of levitation)
- Unique interactions with multiple items (e.g. *this* triggers *that*)
- Different animations (based on the weapon)\[e.g. Cleave vs. Stab] -> description charting
- That items can be prepared in advance (Arrows, Potions, etc.)
- Inventory Management

What I do not like about items from video games:
- Enhancing a non-unique thing an item does past the regular bounds (Ex. the reforge system in Terraria)
- Item differences that are not unique or measured (see the Sandhawk from Borderlands 2)
- basic interactions with multiple items (e.g. having poison makes you do 30% more damage)
- Insufficient/immobile storage for items (e.g. Minecraft chests)
- Restrictions on storage itself (e.g. Minecraft chests) \[not size/slots]
