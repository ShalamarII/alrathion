My hexcrawl based TTRPG system is meant to be a lifetime journey. I want it to evolve with the person I am, the person I have been, and the person I want to be.

# TTRPGs and Me.
My hexcrawl TTRPG is meant to be a deep framework for creating places, people, and items. I do not want to create a roguelike, I want to create a living, breathing world where you can jump through time, create, and shape it. I want the world created by it to be representable if you want to take it elsewhere, such as to a novel, game or other TTRPG system. Ultimately I want to create a storytelling framework with a RPG that can navigate it.

# Storytelling.
I want to tell a story that can describe life itself. I grew up reading books, playing games and watching movies; the most impactful part of it was feeling like I (or the media) was a part of a greater system, whether that is philosophy, cool super powers, or just a family.

I want a person experiencing the stories created to feel like that too.

#### What a TTRPG Needs
A TTRPG has always felt like a unique method of creating stories. A multi-layered approach to finding a story. A DM creates a world to be interacted with, to roam, players create a distillation of a part of themselves to trek that world (or they create a random character in which they distill actions into).

###### Mechanics needed
- Movement (Combat/In Story)
- Encumbrance (or lack of it)
- Inventory
- Resting
- Character Creation
- Skills (Interaction)
- Abilities (Interaction)
- Progression

##### Standard Mechanics (Not Necessary)
- Randomness (Outcomes)

## Philosophy of a TTRPG
This philosophy needs to take a significant portion from cRPG's as I want the gameplay to feel tactical but not overwhelming (similar to Fire Emblem). This is part of the reason I'm taking a cards based approach for abilities and skills. It maximizes expandability, addendums, erratas, etc. It also gives a physical representation of the abilities and spells you have.
